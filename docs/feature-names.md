## Rule 4 record, 2026-11-23

This is a naming-policy check, not legal advice or trademark clearance. It follows the record of the
suite's own check (craft-contrib PR 61).

**What was done.** Names that one company might be known for, and the engine's own name, were checked
one by one (the table below):
- the owners' trademark lists: Unity's (unity.com/legal/trademarks), the Godot Foundation's policy,
  and Adobe's *Trademark List for General Distribution* (dated 21 May 2026);
- the US register through Trademarkia, a USPTO mirror, each name plain, quoted and with spaces removed
  (page 1 of each search, and more pages where noted);
- descriptive use by unrelated products, from their documentation and web search.

**Departures from the SHOULD in rule 4.**
- Every other name in the table above is ordinary words or a standard term for what it does (Copy,
  Delete, Gravity, Square Root, Up, Down): "pass, ordinary words" under rule 1. These names were not
  searched in a register one by one.
- WIPO, TMview and EUIPO were not searched: they refuse scripted requests, as in the suite's own record.
- Epic Games' trademark page could not be read (HTTP 403).
- The Canadian register was not searched for Bayleaf.

Finding nothing does not mean a name is free.
